# How Trump Survived Decades of Legal Trouble:<br>Deny, Deflect, Delay, and Don't Put Anything in Writing
From 1973 and until he was elected president in 2016, Donald Trump and his businesses were involved in over 4,000 legal cases in United States federal and state courts, including battles with casino patrons, million-dollar real estate lawsuits, personal defamation lawsuits, and over 100 business tax disputes.[1] He has also been accused of sexual harassment and sexual assault,[2][3] with one accusation resulting in him being held civilly liable.

It was 1973 and the Justice Department had just filed a civil rights lawsuit against Trump and his father Fred Trump. The complaint alleged that the Trumps and their company, which managed some 14,000 apartments in Queens, Brooklyn and Staten Island, had violated the Fair Housing Act by systematically flagging the applications of Black renters and steering them away from available units.


During the 1950s, Donald Trump was a child, but his father, Fred Trump, was a prominent New York real 
estate developer who faced intense legal and political scrutiny regarding his housing projects.

The notable legal issues and controversies involving the Trump family business during the 1950s include:

1. The 1954 Senate Investigation into FHA Profiteering
In July 1954, Fred Trump was called to testify before a U.S. Senate Banking Committee investigation into widespread corruption and profiteering within the Federal Housing Administration (FHA).

  - The Allegations: Fred Trump was accused of inflating the construction costs of his postwar middle-class housing projects—most notably Beach Haven and Shore Haven in Brooklyn—to obtain massive government-backed loans. He then pocketed the surplus funds.
  - The Outcome: Under oath, Fred Trump admitted that he had pocketed an estimated $1.7 million in excess loan funds. While his actions were highly unethical and resulted in him being blacklisted by the FHA from obtaining future federal housing loans, they were technically legal due to a loophole in the original FHA regulations.
  
2. Beach Haven Tenant Lawsuits
As a direct consequence of the 1954 Senate hearings, tenants living in Fred Trump's Beach Haven complex launched a collective lawsuit against him.
  - The Allegations: Tenants argued that because Trump had artificially inflated his construction expenses to maximize his government windfall, their monthly rent rolls were unfairly high.
  - The Outcome: The tenants sued for rent rollbacks to align their housing costs with the actual money spent on building the complex.
  
3. Early Foundations of Housing Bias (Woody Guthrie)
While the landmark Department of Justice racial discrimination lawsuit against Fred and Donald Trump didn’t formally occur until 1973, the patterns that led to it were established during the 1950s.
  - In December 1950, legendary folk singer Woody Guthrie signed a lease and moved into the Beach Haven apartments.
  - Appalled by the total lack of minority tenants and the overt segregation within Fred Trump's properties, Guthrie wrote an unrecorded song in 1954 titled "Old Man Trump," accusing his landlord of drawing a "color line" and actively stirring up racial hatred.

Other complaints against Trump
In 1988, the Justice Department sued Trump for violating procedures related to public notifications when buying voting stock in a company related to his attempted takeovers of Holiday Corporation and Bally Manufacturing Corporation in 1986. Trump agreed to pay $750,000 to settle the civil penalties of the antitrust lawsuit.[30]

In 2016, the American Bar Association (ABA) commissioned a report to highlight Trump's excess litigation, entitled "Donald J. Trump is a Libel Bully but also a Libel Loser", but initially declined to publish it fearing being sued by Trump.[31][32][33]

In 2001, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission brought a financial-reporting case against Trump Hotels & Casino Resorts Inc., alleging that the company had committed several "misleading statements in the company's third-quarter 1999 earnings release". Trump Hotels & Casino Resorts Inc. consented to the commission's cease-and-desist order, said the culprit had been dismissed, and that Trump had personally been unaware of the matter.[34][35][36]

Trump complaints against his own administration
Before becoming president a second time, Trump filed claim against the Department of Justice, asking for $230 million to compensate him for the FBI investigation that found official and classified documents at Mar-a-Lago. In 2026, he filed a lawsuit against his own Department of the Treasury, asking for $10 billion in compensation for a leak of his tax returns during his first term.[37]
