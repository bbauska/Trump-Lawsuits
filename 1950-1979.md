# 1950's
## US Senate Investigation (1954): 
  + Fred Trump was investigated by the U.S. Senate for profiting off government-backed FHA (Federal Housing Administration) loans by inflating construction and appraisal costs on his housing projects.

## Woody Guthrie Criticisms: 
  + Folk singer Woody Guthrie lived in Fred Trump’s Beach Haven apartment complex in Brooklyn in the 1950s and wrote a song criticizing "Old Man Trump" for his rental and racial practices, though this was a lyrical protest rather than a formal lawsuit.

## No Joint 1950s Lawsuits with Donald: 
	Donald Trump was a child/teenager during the 1950s (born in 1946) and was not involved in lawsuits with his father at that time.

# 1960's
## State Funding Scandals & Investigations
	New York State Investigation Commission (1966): Following the 1954 federal Senate investigation, Fred Trump faced state-level scrutiny in January 1966. He was subpoenaed and forced to testify before the New York State Investigation Commission.

	- The Allegations: Investigators discovered Fred Trump had heavily inflated construction and equipment costs for the state-funded Trump Village project in Coney Island. For example, he billed the state $21,000 to lease a dump truck that was valued at $3,600 and charged $8,280 for tile scrapers that cost $500. Through these padded expenses, he allegedly pocketed over $598,000 in unspent state funds.

	- The Outcome: No criminal charges were filed, but Fred Trump was forced to return $1.2 million to New York State. The investigation severely damaged his reputation, effectively blocking him from securing future public subsidies for major residential projects.

	- Campaign Finance Scandal (1961): Fred Trump was implicated in a minor public scandal when it was revealed he and a group of state contractors contributed to a secret $25,000 campaign fund for New York City mayoral candidate Robert F. Wagner Jr. After the press exposed the money as an attempt to curry political favor for housing contracts, the mayor returned the funds.

# 1970's
The 1970s marked the transition of power from Fred Trump to Donald Trump (who became president of the company in 1971), bringing their business into direct legal conflict with the federal government and state regulators.

## The Evolution of the Housing Bias Claims

	- Root of the 1973 Lawsuit: While the Department of Justice did not officially sue the Trumps until 1973, federal and state records indicate that civil rights activists and the New York City Human Rights Division began documenting complaints and gathering evidence of racial discrimination at Trump properties throughout the 1960s.
	- Donald's Entry: Donald Trump graduated from the Wharton School in 1968 and immediately entered his father's company full-time. It was during these final years of the 1960s that he began managing the properties alongside Fred Trump, inheriting the operational guidelines that triggered the federal lawsuit just a few years later.

## The Major 1970s Civil Rights Lawsuit (Father and Son)

	- Justice Department Lawsuit (1973): The U.S. Department of Justice (DoJ) Civil Rights Division filed a major federal civil rights lawsuit against Fred Trump, his son Donald Trump (who had become company president in 1971), and Trump Management Inc..

	- The Charges: The suit alleged that the Trumps violated the Fair Housing Act of 1968 by discriminating against Black renters, marking applications with a "C" for "colored," and steering Black and Puerto Rican applicants away from buildings with mostly white tenants.

	- Counterclaim: Defended by attorney Roy Cohn, the Trumps countersued the government for $100 million, claiming defamation.

	- Consent Decree (1975): The parties settled the case with a consent decree in which the Trumps agreed not to discriminate, provided a weekly list of vacancies to the New York Urban League, and signed without admitting any guilt.
